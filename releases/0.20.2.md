Kitewell 0.20.2 is a beta update.

## What's in this release

- Renewing a client certificate keeps its sites. **Replace…** on a
  certificate under **Browser** in Device settings takes its renewal and
  keeps the sites it opens. Adding the renewal as a new certificate used to
  be refused, because the old one still held those sites. When a site renews
  your certificate through the browser, Kitewell finds the renewal in your
  keychain, says so in the expiry notice, and opens **Replace…** with it
  chosen. A run that failed because a site's certificate expired offers the
  replacement right where it says so.
- When the assistant opens a site whose certificate has expired, it asks you
  for the renewal on its certificate card and opens the site again once the
  renewal is in place.
- The assistant reads more of the pages you point it to. A site that asks for
  a client certificate, or whose certificate comes from your organization's
  own authority, no longer ends in a TLS error: the assistant says which
  certificate opens it, or opens it in its browser, which presents this
  Mac's certificates. Intranet pages go to that browser too, so a workflow
  for an intranet portal is built from the page itself rather than from your
  description alone.
- On an IPv6-only network, the assistant's page reader and MCP event delivery
  now reach public sites that have only IPv4 addresses. They refused every
  such site.
- The assistant keeps working until the work is done. It used to stop after
  24 actions in a row, on a reply cut short, and after a failed call to the
  model, and wait for you to press **Continue**. It now goes on by itself,
  trying again after a short wait when a call fails, until the work is done,
  needs you, or has gone on for 30 minutes, and then says so.
- The assistant fixes the right step of a long workflow. It reads that step
  on its own, with the loop it runs in, instead of paging through the whole
  definition.
- Workflows the assistant writes no longer fail on two mistakes. A step in a
  loop that read its item as `${item.name}`, which the loop leaves as
  written, now reads `${foreach.item.name}`. A browser step told to find a
  row by a variable, such as "the row whose date is %date%", gets the date
  itself, since the browser's model sees only the variable's name. A secret
  stays hidden. What cannot be fixed this way is named when the workflow is
  checked, before it runs.

## Upgrading

Nothing to do. Kitewell 0.20.1 and 0.20.0 update themselves to 0.20.2 at a
quiet moment, and running workflows go on across the restart.
